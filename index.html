Amino Acids, Peptides & Acid–Base Behavior

Study time · 14 min

▪Structure and Chemistry

GroupMembers
EssentialHistidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine
Exclusively ketogenicLeucine, lysine
Glucogenic and ketogenicIsoleucine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, tyrosine
Acidic side chainsAspartate, glutamate
Basic side chainsLysine, arginine; histidine supports physiologic proton transfer
ResidueStructural role
CysteineDisulfide linkage
GlycineSmallest; crucial to collagen
ProlineRestricts backbone flexibility
  • Peptide bonds are planar with partial double-bond character; polypeptides have N- and C-termini.

▪Isoelectric Point and Titration

Isoelectric point (pI)
pH of zero net charge
Below pI: more positive. Above pI: more negative.
Simple amino acid
pI = (pKa₁ + pKa₂) / 2
Average the two pKa values bracketing the zwitterion
Buffer capacity
Maximal near pKa
Pick the pKa values that bracket net-zero charge
  • Must know: compare buffer pH with pI to predict charge and electrophoretic direction.

▪Pathway Map and Energetics

StepHigh-yield fact
tRNA chargingaa + tRNA + ATP → aa-tRNA + AMP + PPi (aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase); 2 high-energy phosphates
Peptidyl transferCatalyzed by rRNA
Elongation2 GTP per amino acid
Total~4 high-energy phosphates per residue (excluding initiation/termination)
Cytosolic ribosomesMany soluble proteins
Rough ER ribosomesSecreted, membrane and endomembrane proteins
  • Phenylalanine → tyrosine by phenylalanine hydroxylase with BH4, O₂, Fe²⁺, mainly in liver; BH4 is recycled. Supplementary
  • No single rate-limiting enzyme governs all translation; initiation, substrate supply and growth signaling set flux.

▪Regulation and Integration

  • Insulin and amino-acid/mTORC1 signaling support protein synthesis; cortisol may enhance muscle proteolysis under prolonged stress.
  • Fasting promotes hepatic amino-acid catabolism and gluconeogenesis; nutrition and resistance exercise support muscle protein synthesis.
  • No universal allosteric regulator exists for peptide bonds; ATP/GTP and translation factors are the meaningful controls.
Board trapPLP (B6) supports transaminases and decarboxylases, not the ribosomal peptide-bond reaction.

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • Leucine, lysine = purely ketogenic  vs  Ile, Phe, Thr, Trp, Tyr = glucogenic and ketogenic
  • Aspartate, glutamate = acidic  vs  Lysine, arginine = basic
  • Below pI = net positive  vs  Above pI = net negative
  • rRNA = forms the peptide bond  vs  PLP = transamination, not peptide bonds
  • PKU = Phe ↑, PAH test  vs  Alkaptonuria = homogentisate ↑, dark urine
  • MSUD = alloisoleucine  vs  Homocystinuria = total homocysteine, lens dislocation

  • Acidic vs basic side chains; roles of Cys, Gly, Pro?
  • Calculate pI and predict charge at a given pH?
  • Cost of adding one amino acid (~4 high-energy bonds)?
  • Which hormones push protein synthesis vs breakdown?
  • PKU, MSUD, homocystinuria, alkaptonuria: block, buildup, clue, test?

Higher-Order Structure & Misfolding

Study time · 11 min

▪Levels of Structure

LevelDominant stabilizing feature
PrimaryPeptide bonds
SecondaryBackbone hydrogen bonds: α-helices, β-sheets
TertiaryHydrophobic, ionic, H-bond, van der Waals and sometimes disulfide interactions
QuaternaryInter-subunit association, sometimes covalent disulfides
  • Primary sequence shapes higher-order folding and function of globular and fibrous proteins.
  • Disulfide bonds stabilize many extracellular proteins but are not present in all proteins.
  • Hydrophobic collapse buries nonpolar side chains in many soluble proteins.
Board trapDenaturation disrupts higher-order conformation without cleaving peptide bonds; primary structure survives.

▪Folding Pathway and Compartments

Nascent chain→Hsp70-assisted folding→Native protein, or triage to refolding / degradation
SystemRole
Hsp70Assists folding of nascent chains
Hsp60-type chaperoninsATP-dependent folding of selected proteins; prominent in mitochondria
ER quality controlMonitors secreted and membrane proteins
ERADSends irreversibly misfolded ER proteins to the proteasome
Ubiquitin E1Activation consumes ATP
26S proteasomeATP-dependent recognition and unfolding; degrades tagged proteins
  • No single rate-limiting enzyme or fixed net ATP yield defines global proteostasis.

▪Regulation and Integration

  • HSF1 increases heat-shock protein transcription during stress.
  • The unfolded-protein response attenuates translation, induces chaperones and removes misfolded proteins.
  • Insulin/growth signaling raises translational load; energy depletion impairs ATP-dependent folding.
  • PPersistent misfolded protein promotes inflammation, stress signaling and apoptosis.

▪Disease States

DisorderMechanismClueTest
Prion diseasePathogenic PrP conformation templates further misfoldingRapidly progressive dementia, ataxiaCSF RT-QuIC (prion-seeding activity)
Alzheimer diseaseAmyloid-β and phosphorylated tau pathologyProgressive cognitive declineValidated CSF biomarker profile or amyloid PET
Board trapPrion and Alzheimer disease have no single deficient enzyme and no classical substrate/product pair. Damage comes from toxic misfolding and proteostasis failure, not loss of protein synthesis.

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • Secondary structure = backbone H-bonds  vs  Tertiary structure = side-chain interactions
  • Denaturation = loses shape, peptide bonds intact  vs  Hydrolysis = cleaves peptide bonds
  • Hsp70 = nascent-chain folding  vs  Hsp60 chaperonin = ATP-driven folding chamber, mitochondria
  • ERAD = ER misfolded proteins → proteasome  vs  UPR = stress signaling program
  • Prion disease = RT-QuIC  vs  Alzheimer disease = CSF Aβ/tau or amyloid PET

▪Final 5-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • Stabilizing force at each structural level?
  • What denaturation does and does not break?
  • Folding helpers: Hsp70, Hsp60, ER quality control, ERAD, proteasome?
  • HSF1 vs unfolded-protein response?
  • Prion vs Alzheimer: mechanism and test?

Protein Determination & Analytical Techniques

Study time · 9 min

▪Structure and Chemistry

  • Analytical behavior depends on molecular mass, net charge, pI, affinity and structural integrity.
  • Edman degradation identifies N-terminal residues one by one; Sanger FDNB identifies the N-terminal residue after derivatization and hydrolysis.
  • SDS-PAGE separates denatured proteins mainly by mass; isoelectric focusing separates by pI.
  • Mass spectrometry measures mass-to-charge (m/z) signatures of peptides and proteins.

▪Methods and Separation Principles

MethodSeparation principleClinical use
Ion exchangeChargeFractionation
Size exclusionHydrodynamic size; larger elute earlierAggregates / molecular size
AffinitySpecific ligand bindingTarget purification
Serum protein electrophoresisMigration / charge patternMonoclonal gammopathy
ImmunofixationImmunoglobulin identityMonoclonal subtype
Mass spectrometrym/zProtein and amyloid typing
Specimen collection→Protein isolation→Separation→Detection / quantification→Clinical interpretation

▪Practical Rules

  • Electrophoretic resolution depends on buffer pH and ionic strength; sample preparation can change mobility.
  • Reducing agents cleave disulfide bonds in reducing SDS-PAGE and change banding patterns.
  • Compare buffer pH with the protein’s pI before predicting migration direction.
  • Salt gradients elute proteins from ion-exchange media by electrostatic competition.
  • Hemolysis, interference or protein degradation can mislead separation results.
  • These procedures have no biological rate-limiting enzyme, hormonal switch or ATP yield.

▪Diagnostic Correlations

  • DMonoclonal gammopathy: SPEP may show an M band; immunofixation identifies the monoclonal immunoglobulin.
  • DAmyloidosis: Congo red-positive tissue needs amyloid typing; mass spectrometry supports accurate typing.
  • DHemoglobin variants: HPLC or capillary electrophoresis supports sickle and thalassemia evaluation.
Board trapProtein measurements alone do not establish multiple myeloma without integrated clinical and marrow findings.

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • SDS-PAGE = mass  vs  Isoelectric focusing = pI
  • Edman = sequential N-terminal residues  vs  Sanger FDNB = single N-terminal residue
  • Size exclusion = large proteins elute first  vs  Ion exchange = separates by charge
  • SPEP = shows an M band  vs  Immunofixation = identifies its type
  • Buffer pH < pI = protein positive, moves to cathode Supplementary  vs  Buffer pH > pI = protein negative, moves to anode Supplementary

▪Final 5-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • Which property each method separates by?
  • Edman vs Sanger?
  • Predict migration from buffer pH vs pI?
  • Effect of reducing agents on SDS-PAGE?
  • Lab path for monoclonal gammopathy, amyloidosis and Hb variants?

Hemoglobin & Chemistry of Respiration

Study time · 16 min

▪Structure and Chemistry

  • Adult HbA = α₂β₂ with heme Fe²⁺. T state binds O₂ less avidly; R state more avidly.
  • Cooperative binding gives the sigmoid O₂–Hb curve.
  • 2,3-BPG stabilizes deoxy-Hb (T state), lowers O₂ affinity and promotes tissue unloading.
  • HbF (α₂γ₂) binds 2,3-BPG poorly, so it has higher O₂ affinity than HbA.

▪CO₂ Transport Map

Tissue CO₂ enters RBC→CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (carbonic anhydrase)→HCO₃⁻ out / Cl⁻ in (AE1, band 3)
  • Lungs reverse the process: bicarbonate re-enters the RBC, converts to CO₂ and is exhaled.
  • Carbonic anhydrase is zinc-dependent; binding and exchange steps generate no ATP.
EffectMeaning
Bohr effect↑ H⁺ / CO₂ → O₂ affinity ↓
Haldane effectDeoxygenated Hb binds and buffers more CO₂/H⁺
Hamburger (chloride) shiftRBC HCO₃⁻ out, Cl⁻ in via band 3

▪Curve Interpretation

Right shift (P50 ↑)Left shift (P50 ↓)
↑ H⁺, CO₂, temperature↓ H⁺, CO₂, temperature
↑ 2,3-BPG↓ 2,3-BPG
More O₂ unloadingMore O₂ affinity
Exercise, fever, acidosisHbF, alkalosis
  • P50 = PO₂ at 50% saturation; high P50 = lower affinity.
  • High altitude can raise RBC 2,3-BPG.
  • Insulin, glucagon and cortisol are not direct regulators of O₂–Hb binding.
Board trapCO both occupies binding sites and shifts the remaining sites left (higher affinity), worsening tissue O₂ delivery.

▪Clinical Correlations

DisorderMechanism → phenotypeSingle confirmatory test
CO poisoningCarboxyhemoglobin ↑ → O₂-carrying impairmentCarboxyhemoglobin by co-oximetry
MethemoglobinemiaFe³⁺ hemoglobin ↑ → cyanosisMethemoglobin by co-oximetry
Congenital methemoglobinemiaCYB5R3 dysfunction → impaired metHb reductionCYB5R3 genetic testing

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • T state = low affinity (deoxy)  vs  R state = high affinity (oxy)
  • Bohr = H⁺/CO₂ change O₂ binding  vs  Haldane = oxygenation changes CO₂ binding
  • Right shift = P50 ↑, unloading  vs  Left shift = P50 ↓, affinity
  • HbA = binds 2,3-BPG  vs  HbF = binds 2,3-BPG poorly
  • Carboxyhemoglobin = CO bound  vs  Methemoglobin = Fe³⁺ iron

▪Final 6-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • T vs R state and why the curve is sigmoid?
  • How 2,3-BPG and HbF change affinity?
  • CO₂ path: carbonic anhydrase → band 3 chloride shift → lungs?
  • Bohr vs Haldane vs Hamburger?
  • Right vs left shift causes, and P50 meaning?
  • CO poisoning and methemoglobinemia: mechanism and test?

Hemoglobinopathies

Study time · 12 min

▪Structure and Chemistry

  • HbS = β-globin Glu6Val from an HBB missense variant; deoxy-HbS polymerizes through abnormal hydrophobic contacts.
  • α-Thalassemia reduces α-globin synthesis, often by gene deletion; β-thalassemia reduces β-globin synthesis.
  • Chain imbalance causes ineffective erythropoiesis and microcytosis.
  • Severe α deficiency forms HbH (β₄) or fetal Hb Bart’s (γ₄) tetramers.

▪Pathway / Compartment Map

Globin gene transcription (erythroid precursor)→Cytosolic translation→Heme–globin assembly→RBC hemoglobin
  • Heme synthesis uses mitochondrial and cytosolic reactions; intact Hb works in RBC cytosol.
Sickling
Deoxygenation→HbS polymerization→RBC deformation→Hemolysis + vaso-occlusion
β-Thalassemia
β-chain production ↓→Unmatched α chains precipitate→Erythroid damage
  • These are structural/synthesis defects, not enzyme deficiencies; there is no rate-limiting step for Hb binding.

▪Regulation and Integration

FactorEffect
Hypoxemia, dehydration, acidosis, inflammationPromote HbS polymerization and crises
HbFInterferes with sickling
HydroxyureaRaises HbF in many patients
γ-to-β globin switchingChanges Hb composition after birth
HormonesDo not reverse the inherited HBB or α-globin defect

▪Diagnosis

DisorderDefectCharacteristic resultTargeted approach
Sickle cell diseaseHbS-containing genotypeHbS fraction; hemolysisHb HPLC / capillary electrophoresis
β-Thalassemia traitReduced β chainsMicrocytosis; HbA2 often ↑Hemoglobin fractionation
α-Thalassemia traitReduced α chainsMicrocytosis; fractionation may be normalHBA1/HBA2 deletion/duplication testing
Hb Bart’s hydropsNo functional α genesFetal severe anemia / hydropsPrenatal α-globin molecular testing
Board trapA microcytic patient with normal Hb fractionation may still have α-thalassemia trait; it needs deletion testing, not electrophoresis.

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • HbS = qualitative defect (Glu6Val)  vs  Thalassemia = quantitative defect (less chain)
  • α-Thalassemia = often deletions; normal fractionation possible  vs  β-Thalassemia trait = HbA2 ↑
  • HbH = β₄  vs  Hb Bart’s = γ₄
  • Dehydration, acidosis = promote sickling  vs  HbF / hydroxyurea = inhibit sickling

▪Final 5-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • HbS mutation and why deoxy-HbS polymerizes?
  • α vs β thalassemia: mechanism and microcytosis?
  • HbH vs Hb Bart’s composition?
  • Sickling triggers and protective factors?
  • Best test for each disorder, including normal-fractionation α trait?

Enzyme Kinetics, Inhibitors & Cardiac Markers

Study time · 17 min

▪Chemistry and Classification

  • Seven enzyme classes: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases, translocases.
  • Enzymes lower activation energy without changing the equilibrium constant.
  • Km = [S] at half Vmax; Vmax = limiting velocity at saturation.
  • Km is not automatically binding affinity; kcat/Km describes efficiency in suitable systems.

▪Kinetic Equations

Michaelis–Menten
v = Vmax[S] / (Km + [S])
At [S] = Km, v = Vmax/2
Lineweaver–Burk
1/v = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax
x-intercept = −1/Km; y-intercept = 1/Vmax
  • Allosteric enzymes can show sigmoidal curves; plotting enzyme activity yields no ATP.

▪Inhibitor Patterns

InhibitorApparent KmVmaxLineweaver–Burk
Competitive↑SameSame y-intercept
Pure noncompetitiveSame↓Same x-intercept
Uncompetitive↓↓Parallel lines
Mixed↑ or ↓↓Both intercepts change
  • Competitive inhibition can be overcome by more substrate under ideal reversible conditions.
Board trapIrreversible inhibitors remove active enzyme and should not be confused with pure noncompetitive inhibition.

▪Regulation

  • Allosteric binding, covalent phosphorylation and product feedback regulate specific enzyme systems.
  • Insulin, glucagon, epinephrine and cortisol alter specific pathways, not Michaelis–Menten parameters in general.
  • Feedback inhibition and transcriptional control match metabolic supply to demand.

▪Myocardial Injury Markers

MarkerUse and limitation
High-sensitivity cardiac troponin I/TPreferred injury biomarker; not an enzyme
CK-MBCardiac-enriched CK isoenzyme; historical / limited adjunct; normalizes sooner than troponin
MyoglobinEarly but poorly cardiac-specific
AST / LDHHistorical, nonspecific
  • DAcute MI needs a rise/fall in troponin plus evidence of acute ischemia; troponin alone is not enough.
  • PNon-ischemic injury (myocarditis, sepsis, tachyarrhythmia, renal dysfunction) can also raise troponin.
  • Serial troponin, symptoms, ECG and imaging are read together.

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • Competitive = Km ↑, Vmax same; y-intercept shared  vs  Noncompetitive = Vmax ↓, Km same; x-intercept shared
  • Uncompetitive = both ↓, parallel lines  vs  Mixed = Vmax ↓, Km either way
  • Enzyme = lowers activation energy  vs  Equilibrium constant = unchanged
  • Troponin = preferred, not an enzyme  vs  CK-MB = enzyme, earlier normalization
  • Irreversible inhibitor = destroys active enzyme  vs  Noncompetitive inhibitor = reversible, Km unchanged

▪Final 6-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • Name the seven enzyme classes?
  • Define Km and Vmax; v at [S] = Km?
  • Read intercepts off a Lineweaver–Burk plot?
  • Inhibitor type from Km/Vmax changes, including mixed?
  • Why troponin rise alone is not an MI diagnosis?
  • Where CK-MB, myoglobin and AST/LDH fit today?

Collagen Synthesis & Connective-Tissue Disease

Study time · 16 min

▪Structure and Chemistry

  • Collagen repeats Gly–X–Y; glycine must be every third residue for tight triple-helix packing.
TypeLocation / role
IBone, tendon, skin
IICartilage, vitreous
IIIVascular and reticular tissues
IVBasement membranes; forms a network, not fibrils
VRegulates fibril assembly
VIIAnchoring fibrils linking epidermis and dermis
XVIITransmembrane, hemidesmosome-associated

▪Biosynthetic Map

Inside the cell (rough ER)
Prepro-α chains→Pro-α chains→Hydroxylation + glycosylation→Triple-helical procollagen
Outside the cell
Procollagen secreted→N-/C-proteinases cleave propeptides→Tropocollagen→Lysyl oxidase cross-links

▪Regulation and Integration

  • PVitamin C deficiency impairs hydroxylation and triple-helix stability; copper deficiency impairs cross-linking.
  • TGF-β stimulates collagen genes; glucocorticoids may impair synthesis and wound repair.
  • Tissue oxygen, growth factors, amino acids and nutrition influence matrix deposition.
Board trapDistinguish ER hydroxylation (vitamin C) from extracellular cross-linking (copper, lysyl oxidase).

▪Classic Board Distractor Pairs

  • Vitamin C = hydroxylation, ER  vs  Copper = cross-linking, extracellular
  • Type I = bone; OI  vs  Type III = vessels; vascular EDS
  • Type IV = basement membrane network; Alport  vs  Type VII = anchoring fibrils; dystrophic EB

  • Procollagen = inside cell, propeptides on  vs  Tropocollagen = outside cell, propeptides cleaved

▪Final 6-Item PLE Mental Checklist

  • Why glycine every third residue?
  • Location of collagen types I, II, III, IV, V, VII, XVII?

  • Cofactors for hydroxylases vs lysyl oxidase?

Final Sprint

TOS priority closing sequence

▪Key Biochemical Points

  • pI predicts charge and electrophoretic behavior. Competitive inhibition: Km ↑, Vmax same. Pure noncompetitive: Vmax ↓, Km same.
  • Hb T state = lower O₂ affinity. Bohr = H⁺/CO₂ effect on O₂ binding; Haldane = oxygenation effect on CO₂ carriage; Hamburger = RBC HCO₃⁻/Cl⁻ exchange.
  • HbS = β6 Glu→Val; thalassemia reduces globin chain synthesis.
  • Troponin is the preferred cardiac injury marker but is not an enzyme.
  • Vitamin C enables collagen hydroxylation; copper enables lysyl oxidase cross-linking.

▪Key Clinical Chains

  • PPKU: PAH block → phenylalanine ↑ → developmental injury → PAH molecular confirmation.
  • PSickle disease: HbS polymerization → hemolysis / vaso-occlusion → hemoglobin fractionation.
